IRRIGATION WAITING FOR DATA
Collecting data...

From data to irrigation decisions.

The smart irrigation system combines multiple sources of information about the farm to determine irrigation needs.

Soil moisture data provides information about the current conditions in the soil, while weather data provides environmental conditions and forecasts. Water-level sensors monitor available water in storage tanks.

These datasets are brought together through an algorithm that analyses the conditions and supports a decision on whether irrigation is required.

DATA & REMOTE SENSING

Multiple data sources. One connected system.

📡

Remote sensing

Satellite and drone imagery provide information about vegetation and crop conditions through products such as NDVI and NDRE.

📶

Field sensors

Soil-moisture sensors, weather stations and water-level sensors provide real-time information about conditions on the farm.

🗺️

Spatial analysis

GIS combines the different datasets with farm boundaries, infrastructure and terrain to provide spatial context for irrigation decisions.

Planning phase research

The Journey Begins

Starting in the planning phase of the project. The project team explores the University of Pretoria Hatfield campus insearch of data sources to utilize in our project. We spoke to lecturers and department heads in fields relevant to our project to find free to use sensors for data collection. In this search we came accross the meteorology department which houses an Automatic Weather Station (AWS) ontop of the geography building. We recieved permission to view the AWS and its where we took our first project team picture.

Planning phase research continues

Sensor sponsor found

In our exploration efforts we found a potential source of soil moisture sensors we need for our project. Our team visited the Agricultural department and met with Dr Nicolette J Taylor. We found out that a team of phd students were working on a project where they monitor soil moisture in the peacan nut orchards at the University of Pretoria farm called Farm@UP. We were able to get permission to use their sensors data for our project and we are very grateful for their support.
